Folly Beach, SC · Bachelorette · Food · Water Sports · Nightlife

Folly Beach Bachelorette Guide | Plan the Perfect Beach Bash

Planning a bachelorette party on Folly Beach? You get real surf, calm marsh creeks full of dolphins, a walkable strip of restaurants and bars on Center Street, and downtown Charleston about 20 minutes away. Here's where to eat, how to get on the water, where to go out, and the tips that keep the weekend easy.

Where to Eat on Folly Beach

Folly is casual, so nobody needs a dress code. Most kitchens sit on or just off Center Street, within a few blocks of the pier. Weekend waits are real in season, so put a name in early or split into smaller tables.

Breakfast & Brunch

Lost Dog Café is the classic first stop. Breakfast runs all day, with eggs Benedict, breakfast burritos, waffles, and bagel sandwiches, and the patio is dog-friendly. The line gets long on weekends, so go early. If it's packed, walk to Black Magic Café for shrimp and cheese grits.

Lowlife Bar, just off Center Street, serves brunch every day of the week. Order the chipotle shrimp tacos and Old Bay waffle fries, and try a frozen spiced piña colada for the bride. For a view, Pier 101 sits right on the Folly Beach Pier. It serves breakfast daily, and the outdoor tables look straight out over the Atlantic.

Lunch Between Beach Sessions

Taco Boy is a Folly icon a block from the sand. Expect tacos like buttermilk fried chicken, kimchi beef, and Baja fried fish, plus street corn and an outdoor patio. A DJ takes over later at night. Chico Feo is a Caribbean-style shanty with outdoor seating only and a menu that changes with the season. Look for creative tacos, Vietnamese dishes, goat curry, and live music.

Rita's Seaside Grille sits across from the pier at 2 Center Street. It has a big patio, weekend brunch, award-winning chili, and frequent live music. The Washout keeps it simple, with affordable beach food on an outdoor patio.

Dinner & Seafood

Pinky's on the Beach took over the old BLU spot in the Tides hotel and is the island's only true oceanfront restaurant. It's all pink, from the Cadillac booths to the pool table, which makes it an easy bachelorette photo. Order the chilled crab dip, calamari fries, seafood baskets, and frozen cocktails.

Folly Beach Crab Shack is the go-to for crab legs, shrimp, and fresh fish, with live local bands during peak season. Jack of Cups Saloon is a quirky Center Street favorite with bold, globally inspired dishes and craft drinks.

Worth the Short Drive

Bowens Island Restaurant is a no-frills seafood spot on its own island off Folly Road, and it's the area's go-to for sunset over the marsh. Lolo's is a small house on the Folly River about a mile before the bridge, with water views from every table. Both work well for a relaxed first-night dinner.

Coffee & Sweets

Grab coffee at Roasted inside the Tides hotel, open early for beach-day runs. For dessert, walk to Dolce Banana Café for ice cream.

Water Sports & Boat Days

Folly is the surf capital of South Carolina, and the Folly River behind the island is calm, shallow marsh that's ideal for first-time paddlers. Plan water time for the morning, when wind and water are calmest, and save the boat for late afternoon.

Surf Lessons

Folly's famous break is The Washout, a stretch of East Ashley Avenue where Hurricane Hugo wiped out a block of beachfront homes. It's the most consistent wave in the area, but it gets crowded and is best left to intermediate surfers. Beginners do better at 10th Street East or other quieter stretches on a small day. In summer the water runs around 82 degrees and waves usually stay in the one- to three-foot range.

Book a group lesson with Shaka Surf School, which works out of 6th Street East, or Isla Surf School, which teaches all levels. Charleston Surf Co offers private 90-minute lessons, plus eFoil sessions for anyone who wants to fly over the water. For rentals, McKevlin's Surf Shop at 8 Center Street has been open since 1965 and is one of the oldest surf shops in the country. Ocean Surf Shop at 31 Center Street is the other local favorite.

Paddleboarding & Kayaking

Charleston SUP Safaris runs guided paddleboard and kayak tours on the Folly River, with morning, afternoon, and sunset dolphin trips. Dolphins are common in these creeks. For something different, ask about full-moon paddles or SUP yoga. Rentals range from two hours to a full day, start at 10 a.m., and include life jackets.

Wind Land & Water Sports at 109 East Ashley Avenue rents paddleboards, surfboards, boogie boards, skimboards, and e-bikes. It's a one-stop shop if half the group wants to paddle and the other half wants to ride the waves. Coastal Expeditions also launches kayak trips from Folly.

Private Boat Charters

A private boat is the best group activity on the island. Flipper Finders, based on Center Street, runs private three-hour party pontoon charters for up to 18 people, with a cooler and ice included. Their Dolphin Queen holds up to 20 and stops at a nearby beach so you can swim and float. Some boats have no bathroom, so use the office before you board.

For a smaller crew, book a private tiki boat for up to 12, or a sunset sail on a catamaran from the Folly River landing. Morris Island Charters runs private sunset and dolphin cruises from 97 Center Street, and you can bring your own drinks. Many trips include a stop at Morris Island to look for shells and shark teeth near the lighthouse.

Fishing & the Pier

The Folly Beach Pier stretches about 1,000 feet into the Atlantic and is open for fishing, or just a sunset walk. Fishing charters run out of the Folly River for groups that want to cast a line with a captain.

Stay Safe in the Water

Rip currents happen on Folly, so swim near lifeguards when they're posted. Don't swim near the pier pilings, and check the surf report before anyone paddles out. Wear reef-safe sunscreen and a hat, since there's very little shade on the water.

More Daytime Ideas

Book a group spa day to recharge before the night. Then walk out from the east end of the island to see the Morris Island Lighthouse for the group photo.

After Dark

Pack snacks and music for a sunset beach picnic. Fires aren't allowed on the beach.

Book a Private Show with Michael At Your Place

Michael At Your Place is a private, professional male entertainer who comes to your beach house, condo, or hotel for a show built around the bride. It's just your group and your music, and you set the comfort level. Book it as the kickoff before Center Street or the finale after dinner.

Top Bars on Center Street

Folly's nightlife is lively but low-key, and it's all within walking distance. Snapper Jacks is a rooftop with ocean views and DJs, the place to start the night. Loggerhead's Beach Grill has a big outdoor deck with live music and karaoke. The Washout brings loud music and big energy. The Bounty Bar is a beachy spot for dancing and a nightcap. Chico Feo and Lowlife Bar are also great for live music.

Planning Tips

Book Early

Lock in the house first, then Michael's show, dinner reservations, and any boat charters. Spring through fall weekends fill fast. Many charters only give a full refund if you cancel three weeks out.

No Alcohol or Glass on the Beach

Save toasts for the rental, the boat, or the bar. Bring reusable cups.

Getting Around

Uber from downtown to Center Street. Bikes or golf carts work on the island, and paid lots are near the pier. If you park on the street, keep all four tires off the pavement to avoid a ticket.

Where to Stay

A house or condo walkable to Center Street keeps rides cheap and gives the group a home base.

What to Pack

Swimsuits, reef-safe sunscreen, towels, walkable sandals, and matching tees or sashes.

Budget as a Group

Set a per-person number early and split the house evenly.
