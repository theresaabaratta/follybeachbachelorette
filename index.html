Unforgettable Folly Beach Bachelorette Ideas for an Epic Girls' Weekend

November 17, 2026

Here are nine ideas to make your Folly Beach bachelorette the most epic weekend the bride has ever had.

Introduction to Folly Beach

Folly is the laid-back island about 20 minutes from downtown Charleston, and it packs a lot into a few square miles. You get real surf on the ocean side, calm marsh creeks full of dolphins on the river side, and a walkable strip of restaurants and bars on Center Street in between. Nobody needs a dress code, rides stay short, and the whole crew can walk from the rental to the pier. If the group wants a night in the city too, our friends cover downtown in their Party Charleston bachelorette guide.

9 Ideas for a Killer Bachelorette Party on Folly Beach

You can't go wrong with any of these. Plan water time for the morning when wind and waves are calmest, save the boat for late afternoon, and let Center Street handle the rest. Here are nine great ways to spend a bachelorette in Folly Beach.

Center Street Brunch

Lost Dog Café is the classic first stop, with breakfast all day: eggs Benedict, breakfast burritos, waffles, and bagel sandwiches on a dog-friendly patio. Weekend lines get long, so go early. If it's packed, walk to Black Magic Café for shrimp and cheese grits.

Lowlife Bar serves brunch every day of the week. Order the chipotle shrimp tacos and Old Bay waffle fries, plus a frozen spiced piña colada for the bride. For a view, Pier 101 sits right on the Folly Beach Pier with outdoor tables over the Atlantic.

Day at the Beach

It's an island, so the beach is the main event. Set up near the pier, where kitchens sit within a few blocks of the sand, and send a runner for lunch. Taco Boy is a Folly icon a block from the water, with buttermilk fried chicken, kimchi beef, and Baja fish tacos, plus a DJ later at night.

Chico Feo is a Caribbean-style shanty with outdoor seating only, a seasonal menu, goat curry, and live music. Rita's Seaside Grille sits across from the pier at 2 Center Street with a big patio and award-winning chili, and The Washout keeps it simple with affordable beach food.

Surf Lessons

Folly is the surf capital of South Carolina. The famous break is The Washout on East Ashley Avenue, where Hurricane Hugo wiped out a block of beachfront homes. It's the most consistent wave around but best left to intermediates. Beginners do better at 10th Street East on a small day, when summer water runs about 82 degrees and waves stay one to three feet.

Book a group lesson with Shaka Surf School on 6th Street East or Isla Surf School. Charleston Surf Co offers private 90-minute lessons and eFoil sessions. For rentals, McKevlin's Surf Shop at 8 Center Street has been open since 1965, and Ocean Surf Shop at 31 Center Street is the other local favorite.

Paddle With the Dolphins

The Folly River behind the island is calm, shallow marsh that's ideal for first-time paddlers, and dolphins are common in these creeks. Charleston SUP Safaris runs guided paddleboard and kayak tours with morning, afternoon, and sunset dolphin trips. Ask about full-moon paddles or SUP yoga. Rentals run two hours to a full day, start at 10 a.m., and include life jackets.

Wind Land & Water Sports at 109 East Ashley Avenue rents paddleboards, surfboards, boogie boards, skimboards, and e-bikes, so half the group can paddle while the other half rides waves. Coastal Expeditions also launches kayak trips from Folly.

Private Boat Party

A private boat is the best group activity on the island. Flipper Finders on Center Street runs private three-hour party pontoon charters for up to 18, with a cooler and ice included. Their Dolphin Queen holds up to 20 and stops at a nearby beach to swim and float. Some boats have no bathroom, so use the office before you board.

For a smaller crew, book a private tiki boat for up to 12 or a sunset sail on a catamaran from the Folly River landing. Morris Island Charters runs private sunset and dolphin cruises from 97 Center Street, BYOB, and many trips stop at Morris Island to hunt for shells and shark teeth near the lighthouse.

Lighthouse, Pier & Spa Day

Walk out from the east end of the island to the Morris Island Lighthouse for the group photo. The Folly Beach Pier stretches about 1,000 feet into the Atlantic and works for fishing or a sunset stroll, and fishing charters run out of the Folly River for anyone who wants to cast with a captain.

Need to recharge before the night? Book a group spa day. After dark, pack snacks and music for a sunset beach picnic. Fires aren't allowed on the beach.

Sunset Seafood Dinner

Pinky's on the Beach took over the old BLU spot in the Tides hotel and is the island's only true oceanfront restaurant. It's all pink, from the Cadillac booths to the pool table, which makes it the easiest bachelorette photo on Folly. Order the chilled crab dip, calamari fries, and frozen cocktails.

Folly Beach Crab Shack is the go-to for crab legs and shrimp, and Jack of Cups Saloon brings bold, global dishes. Worth the short drive: Bowens Island Restaurant for sunset over the marsh, or Lolo's on the Folly River. Grab morning coffee at Roasted and ice cream at Dolce Banana Café.

Private Show at the Rental

$200 · up to 90 minutes

Michael's place bachelorette show is a private, professional entertainer who comes to your Folly Beach house, condo, or hotel for a show built around the bride. It's just your group and your music, and you set the comfort level.

Shows are $200 for up to 90 minutes, paid by cash, Venmo, or Cash App when he arrives. Book it right after the house, since weekend dates go first.

Check Dates

Bar Crawl & Dancing on Center Street

Folly's nightlife is lively but low-key, and it's all walkable. Start at Snapper Jacks, a rooftop with ocean views and DJs. Move to Loggerhead's Beach Grill for the big outdoor deck, live music, and karaoke. The Washout brings loud music and big energy, and The Bounty Bar is the beachy spot for dancing and a nightcap. Chico Feo and Lowlife Bar are also great for live music.

Planning Tips for the Weekend

Book early. Lock in the house first, then Michael's show, dinner reservations, and any boat charters. Spring through fall weekends fill fast, and many charters only give a full refund if you cancel three weeks out.

No alcohol or glass on the beach. Save toasts for the rental, the boat, or the bar, and bring reusable cups.

Getting around. Uber from downtown to Center Street. Bikes or golf carts work on the island, and paid lots sit near the pier. If you park on the street, keep all four tires off the pavement to avoid a ticket.

Where to stay. A house or condo walkable to Center Street keeps rides cheap and gives the group a home base. Set a per-person budget early and split the house evenly.

Stay safe in the water. Rip currents happen on Folly, so swim near posted lifeguards, stay away from the pier pilings, and check the surf report before anyone paddles out.

What to pack. Swimsuits, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, towels, walkable sandals, and matching tees or sashes.
